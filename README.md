# Bonnie Bounce

A forty-five-level platform adventure starring Bonnie, in the style of a 1999
handheld colour game. Grown out of TEKEX Jump (the Easter egg on the Tekex
site), it's now full screen, in colour, and a whole game: a world map,
power-ups, checkpoints, four bosses and five endings. The fifth land was
designed by Bonnie himself.

> Grumble the Dragon has taken Monsty! Bounce through ten lands to bring him home —
> and find Bear, the family's grey cat, who's hiding somewhere in every one.
>
> Then, at Monsty's birthday party, Captain Magpie swoops in and steals the
> presents. Sail to the Magpie Isles and win them back.
>
> And then it's off to Disneyland Paris in the family car — until Rascal the
> Fox snatches Brocco, Bonnie's broccoli, and runs for Paris.
>
> And then — it's still the holidays: a waterpark, surfing, the bike ride
> where Bonnie learnt to ride, Puy du Fou, Saint-Malo, and the ferry home.
>
> Home again, Bonnie writes his One Day list — everything he'd love to do one
> day. But Queen Bee's bees carry it off, and his wishes flutter out all over
> the world. Fold a paper aeroplane and fly after them.
>
> Then it's autumn half term. At the pumpkin patch Bonnie finds the biggest
> pumpkin ever — and that night a Witch swoops over the moon and magics it
> away for her soup. Follow her trail, trick-or-treating all the way, up to
> her crooked tower.

## Playing it

Double-click `index.html` and it opens in a browser. There's nothing to
install and it doesn't need the internet — it works straight from the folder.

On an iPad or phone, put the folder somewhere the device can reach (or
host it anywhere static) and open `index.html`. **Add to Home Screen**
gives it the whole screen, with Bonnie as the app's icon.

Play it **sideways**. Three big see-through buttons sit over the game,
drawn in its own style: ◀ ▶ under your left thumb and the big bouncy
mushroom (jump) under your right (the whole right half of the screen jumps, and holding
jumps higher). There's no run button — hold a direction and Bonnie
speeds up. With the Super Ball a throw button appears beside JUMP. The
paper button top right pauses; sound is in the pause menu. Menus, the
map and the sticker book are tapped. The buttons fade whenever Bonnie
walks behind them. If nobody touches the screen for a few seconds in a
level, the buttons glow and a see-through finger shows what to press. Held upright it still plays (handy with rotation
lock on), with a small note under the picture suggesting turning
sideways for the best view.

On an iPhone the sound plays even with the ring/silent switch on — it's
a game, it should always have its music. Browsers keep a page silent
until it's tapped, so the first tap on the title screen starts the
music (Level 1's tune) and the next one plays.

**It keeps itself up to date.** Every time it opens, it asks for the
newest files first; if it's been sitting open on a home screen and a new
version has been published, it reloads itself — on the title or the map,
never in the middle of a level. It also keeps a copy, so once it's been
opened it plays with no internet connection too.

### Controls

| | Keyboard | Touch | Gamepad |
|---|---|---|---|
| Move | ← → (or A / D) | ◀ ▶ | D-pad / stick |
| Jump (hold for higher) | Space, Z or ↑ | JUMP (or anywhere on the right) | bottom / right button |
| Run | ⌘ Command, Option, Shift or X | hold ◀ or ▶ | left / top button |
| Throw a ball | the run key | the throw button | left / top button |
| Duck (when big) | ↓ | — | down |
| Pause · start | Enter or Esc | the paper pause button | Start |
| Sound on/off | M | in the pause menu | — |
| Full screen | F | — | — |

Every prompt in the game shows the controls you're actually using — the
Space bar on a keyboard, the on-screen buttons on a touch screen, the
face buttons on a gamepad — and switches the moment you do.

Under the sea, tap jump to swim.

**Getting about the map.** Tap a level to walk there (a long way, and he
hurries through the stops on the way), or tap **GO** (under BOOK, top
left; ↑ on a keyboard) for **WHERE TO?**: every level reached, a row for
each land in its colours, a gold dot on the finished ones. Tap one and
he's there, ready to play — level 10 from the far end of the game in two
taps.

On a Mac, ⌘ + Space opens Spotlight before the browser sees it, so a
running jump with ⌘ held brings up Spotlight. Option does the same job as ⌘
without that clash (or switch off Spotlight's shortcut in System Settings →
Keyboard → Keyboard Shortcuts).

## The game

**Fifty-five worlds in six lands**, each with its own look, music, enemies and trick.
The second island, the Magpie Isles, opens when Monsty is home: a boat
route across the water on the map, and a short party scene to start it.
The third land, the Big Trip, opens when the presents are back: a ferry
over to France, where the family's dark grey Yeti drives Bonnie from
level to level. Bonnie's mum and dad, Lucy and Luke, are in it — waiting by
the car at the end of every level and cheering at every checkpoint — and
so is Brocco, Bonnie's broccoli soft toy. The fifth land, One Day…, opens
when the holiday's over: Bonnie designed its ten levels himself (20 Oct,
aged six) — things he'd like to do one day — and drew some of its cast. A
paper aeroplane flies him across the map and waits at the end of each level.
The sixth land, Half Term!, opens when Queen Bee's said sorry: autumn and a
fun, never-scary Halloween (the Haunted Mansion and Nightmare Before
Christmas were the feel Luke asked for, 23 Oct). Its levels are longer and
a little trickier than the others, with more to find, and each ends at a
crooked spooky-cute house where Bonnie knocks for a sweet.

Bonnie drew these baddies himself: the **squirrel** that throws nuts, the
walking **chocolate bar**, the cross **palm tree**, the **butterfly**, the
**Christmas tree**, the **building** with eyes and the **polar bear** (two
stomps). And his two things for the blocks: the **bullet** and the **heart**.

**Things to find, in every level:** three golden **medals** (they show on
the map, the level's card and the HUD, and win the football kits);
**secret walls** that look solid but walk straight through, with a room
behind (one sat across the way on has a secret wall at each end, so
it's a tunnel, never a dead end); **balloons** that float Bonnie up into the sky for a while;
**musical blocks** that play their note as he lands on or runs across them.

**Hearts:** Bonnie has one (it was three, until he found the game "very
easy"). A bump from a baddie while he's small, or a fall into a pit or
lava, and he starts again — from the checkpoint flag if he reached it,
after a one-second card. Lives are unlimited. When he's big, a bump
shrinks him first, without costing the heart. A **heart** from a present
(Bonnie's idea) gives him a second heart for the rest of the level (or
fills it again): with two, a bump costs one and he carries on, and a
fall bubbles him back to safe ground. Back at a flag, it's one heart
again. `HP_MAX` and `EXTRA_MAX` in `js/game.js` set both (2 a heart).

**Bosses:** landing on top of a boss never hurts. At the right moment
(Grumble any time, the Captain when he's landed, Rascal when he's dizzy,
Queen Bee when she's stuck in her honey, the Witch when she's bumped
down dizzy after a swoop)
it's a hit; at any other it's a bounce. A boss that's just been hit
flashes, and can't hurt Bonnie until he stops. Only touching him from
the side or below, or his fire, eggs and baguettes, costs the heart.

**Bear** hides in every level — up on a stump, a ledge, the top of a
climbing frame. Touch him and he meows and follows Bonnie home (he walks
the very path Bonnie takes, a moment behind, and keeps him company after
a fall). Bring him home and his face lights up on that level's map tag.

**The sticker book** (BOOK, top left of the map, or the run key) has a
sticker of Bear for every land he came home from (a page for each
land), one for every creature Bonnie has met, and the **outfits** —
star pyjamas, a superhero suit with a cape, a Monsty suit, a pirate suit
with a tricorn hat and a golden one with a crown, won by bringing Bear
home 3, 6, 10, 15 and 20 times; a football kit, a golf outfit with a
cap, and mouse ears for a day at the park, won by finishing levels 21,
25 and 29; his two teams' kits — PSG (navy, the red stripe) and
Liverpool (all red), no badges — won with 10 and 25 golden medals; and a
Pokémon trainer's cap, a penguin suit, a dinosaur onesie and a bee
costume, won by finishing levels 36, 40, 44 and 45; and wellies with a
bobble hat, a pumpkin costume, a skeleton suit and the Witch's hat and
cloak, won by finishing levels 46, 49, 51 and 55. Its WHO'S PLAYING?
page lets you play as **Bonnie, Mum or Dad** (Bonnie asked for it): the
other two cheer at the flags and wait at the end. Once the holiday's
over there's a **Pokédex** page for the Pokémon caught in level 36.

**Grown-ups** (title menu or pause menu, hold to open) can record their
own "Yay!", "Wheee!" and "Well done!" — played at checkpoints, on
trampolines and when a level is clear. The clips stay on that device.
Under them, **OPEN LEVEL 36** (or whichever land is next) opens the next
land without finishing the one before: the lands before it count as won,
its story still plays, and nothing else in the save changes. One land
per tap. It's how Bonnie's iPad got to the new levels before he'd
finished the holiday.

**Half Term!'s friends and tricks.** The **ghost dog** with the glowing
pumpkin nose is asleep in Pumpkin Town: wake him and he floats along
behind Bonnie for the rest of the land, lighting the dark (a fall
doesn't lose him). The three **hitchhiking ghosts** in the graveyard
join the line behind him too. A **kite**, a **sycamore seed** or a
**firework rocket** carries him hanging underneath (jump to let go of a
kite); the **broomstick** flies — hold jump to climb, let go to glide —
until its magic fizzles, never over a gap, and it won't sink out of
sight into one (it skims the bottom); a bump knocks him off it (no
heart lost) and it flies home. The Witch's **potions** turn him into a
frog for a few seconds instead of costing his heart (another splash
just keeps him a frog for longer).

**Power-ups** come out of the presents:

- **Cupcake** — Bonnie grows big; he can take a hit and smash bricks.
- **Super Ball** — his jumper turns red and B throws bouncing balls.
- **Rainbow Lolly** — a few seconds of rainbow invincibility.
- **Heart** — a second heart for the rest of the level.
- **Bullet** (Bonnie's) — he surfs it at full speed for a few seconds,
  knocking baddies flying; it rises over anything in the way (or when
  jump's held), and only ever drops him on solid ground.

Every level has a checkpoint flag halfway (`L.check(x)` stands it on
whatever ground is at x, however high — give a row only for a ledge or a
roof that isn't the ground), and ends at an arch of
balloons in front of Bonnie's house (on the Magpie Isles, his tent; on
the Big Trip, the family car with Mum and Dad beside it) — the
higher he runs through it, the more points. Progress saves automatically (in the browser, on that device).

Nothing in it is borrowed: presents, wooden toy blocks and each world's
own columns (tree stumps, a sandcastle tower, coral, crystal rock, cloud
pillars, a factory funnel, a chimney) — and a grumpy sunflower.

## For grown-ups who want to tinker

Everything is plain HTML and JavaScript — no build step.

The game thinks in "game pixels" (8x8 tiles, a 144-pixel-tall view) and
draws each one as 2x2 screen pixels. Sprites are typed at game resolution
and doubled when painted — smoothed, outlined in a deep shade of their own
colour, and lit from the top left — which is what gives the Game Boy
Advance look. Bonnie, the presents and the tiles are drawn at the full
resolution to begin with — Bonnie as a posed puppet (head, then jumper,
sleeves, hands, shorts, legs and shoes on joints), so a new pose is a few
numbers in `POSES` in `js/art.js`.

| File | What's in it |
|---|---|
| `js/core.js` | screen scaling, palette, the sprite painter (doubling, outlines, lighting), pixel font, input, the sound chip |
| `js/art.js` | every sprite (Bonnie is at the top), tiles and the thirty-five themes |
| `js/creatures.js` | every enemy, the three bosses, and the scenery props, in the same round, softly lit style |
| `js/family.js` | Bonnie's family, drawn from photos: Lucy and Luke (Mum and Dad), Brocco, and the family's Skoda Yeti; Bonnie on his bike and surfing |
| `js/backdrop.js` | the parallax backgrounds, layered into the haze |
| `js/light.js` | sunbeams, dusk and glowing lights, shadows — and how Bonnie catches the light |
| `js/music.js` | the soundtrack — all original tunes, written as note strings |
| `js/levels.js` | the level builder, and the first land's ten levels |
| `js/levels2.js` … `levels6.js` | the other lands' levels, one file each, built with the same kit |
| `js/creatures5a.js`, `creatures5b.js` | the fifth land's cast: Bonnie's own designs, the Pokémon, Queen Bee, the dinosaurs, the rides, its scenery |

Handy while editing:

- `index.html?level=7` jumps straight into level 7.
- `index.html?debug` opens every level on the map.
- `tools/sheet.html` shows every sprite, magnified (`?z=8` to zoom).
- `tools/overview.html?level=all` draws every level flat, to check layouts.
- `tools/playtest.html?level=all` sends a robot through every level (or
  `?level=11-20`, `?level=19`), to prove each can be finished after a
  change. It searches short moves — walk, run, jump, hold, wait — always
  carrying on from the spot nearest the end. It starts small with the
  game's one heart, so a bump or a fall ends that try: a level it
  finishes can be finished without a single hit. It also checks every
  level has its three medals (`&medals=1` goes after each of them;
  `&bear=1` goes and finds Bear). All of 1–54 pass (the whole run takes a few minutes; the boss
  fights take longest). If one fails, it says how far it got. After each
  level it also starts again from every checkpoint, as after losing the
  last heart, and checks he can walk off (a flag buried in Windy Hill's
  hill once put him back inside it). The one it
  can't do is the Witch (55): it climbs her tower and gets into her
  kitchen, but her fight means waiting out a long flight with nothing to
  aim at, and its search runs out first. She was beaten (23 Oct) by a
  simple scripted player instead — wait on the middle shelf while she
  loops and swoops, then hop down and stomp her when she's dizzy — and
  her medals and Bear pass as usual. Ghost platforms keep a 160-frame bar
  so they line up with the robot's 320-frame clock.
- `tools/playtest.html?level=46-55&stuck=1` hunts for places he can get
  stuck — somewhere he can get into but not out of. Finishing a level
  proves one way through; this wanders everywhere else (into every
  secret room, onto every ledge, island and roof, baddies taken away and
  nothing able to hurt him), and from every spot he can stand on asks
  whether he can still get on 24 tiles (in the Witch's tower, up five
  rows). It lists the spots he can't, where he can stay alive but not get
  out — a spot he can only fall from is a fall, not a trap, and isn't
  listed. `&spot=273,13` (with `&level=` one level) explains one listed
  spot: what he was stood on, what was near, how far each try got. It
  was made after Bonnie got stuck in a secret room (23 Oct): the land-6
  dens had one secret wall, so walking along the ground took him in and
  the far wall was solid. Every secret room on the way is a tunnel now,
  in one side and out the other. It always heads onwards, so where the
  way on is back behind him (Windy Hill's second kite, a few tiles back
  from the edge) or a slow ride (the buggy coming back) it can run out of
  tries: a listed spot like that is worth checking by hand before it's
  believed. One is listed in land 6, checked by hand: Windy Hill
  323–330 (he walks back, takes the kite and is carried over).
- `index.html?touch` plays as a touch screen does, to check the phone and
  tablet layouts on a desktop (any window shape).
- `tools/icon.html` redraws the home-screen icons; save each into `icons/`.
- How hard it is: `SLOW` (how fast baddies and hazards move, 0.94 now),
  `THIN` (one baddie in THIN is left out, 10 now) and `HEAL_STARS` (stars
  for half a heart, 12 now) at the top of `js/game.js`, next to the jump
  and forgiveness settings; and `HP_MAX`, the hearts (2 a heart, so 2 now
  for one heart, 6 for three), under "hearts" further down.
- Level 1's signposts are `L.tip(x, 'jump')` and so on; the words and
  pictures for each are `TIPS` in `js/game.js`.

### Making a trailer

`tools/reel/` films a 16:9 trailer straight from the game — no HUD, no
words, only play — with the game's own sound effects and the title tune
under it, cut on the music's bars. It made the 29-second LinkedIn video
(19 Oct). The scenes are a list at the top of `tools/reel/reel.js`: a
level, a tile to start at, how long, an outfit, and the beats to happen
in order (find Bear, the balloons, the steam, a surfboard, a catapult, a
secret room and its medal, a baguette jumped and a stomp on Rascal). For
each, the playtest robot finds a run that's never hit and that plays the
way a person would: `STYLE` makes jumps dear (and held once made),
going back and dawdling a little dear, running free — without it the
robot hops about all the time. Luke and Lucy are kept out of shot.
`REEL.sheet(i, 12, 'name')` saves a contact sheet of scene `i` to
`tools/reel/out/name.png`, to check a scene before filming it all.
`tools/reel/?reel=halfterm` films the Half Term! trailer instead (23 Oct,
25 seconds): the leaf pile, the owl, the kite, a trick-or-treat door,
the curly hill, a hitchhiker, the stretching room, the broomstick, a
rocket and a stomp on the Witch, under Pumpkin Town's tune.

1. `node tools/reel/serve.js 8171`, and open `localhost:8171/tools/reel/`
   (it serves the game and saves what the page sends it).
2. In the page's console: `REEL.film()`. About a minute (two, in a
   background tab): the frames and the soundtrack land in
   `tools/reel/out/` (ignored by git).
   `REEL.look(18, 140)` shows a frame of level 18 at tile 140, to choose
   a spot; `REEL.plan(i)` and `REEL.show(i, frame)` try out scene `i`.
3. `swiftc -O -swift-version 5 tools/reel/encode.swift -o /tmp/reel-encode`
   then `/tmp/reel-encode tools/reel/out ~/Desktop/trailer.mp4` — a
   1920x1080, 60fps H.264 MP4 with AAC sound, using only what comes with
   macOS (the Xcode command line tools).

Levels are written with short commands — `L.ground(0, 56)`, `L.row(16, 11, 'BPB?B')`,
`L.medal(x, row)`, `L.secret(x0, y0, x1, y1)`, `L.balloons(x, row)`, `L.notes(x, row, 'CDEFG')`, `L.water(…)`,
`L.pipe(28, 2)` (a column in the world's own style — a stump, a tower, a chimney), `L.e('grumbug', 24)`,
`L.bear(48, 12)` — explained at the top of `js/levels.js`.
To reset the save, clear the site's storage in the browser, or run
`localStorage.removeItem('bonnie-bounce-save')` in its console.
